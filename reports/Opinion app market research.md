# Opinion survives only by winning answerers first

Opinion is viable as a niche product, not as a mass consumer app, and only if the spec changes how it recruits the people who answer. The notes found **no verified product that combines expert or community targeting, required written reasons, a 3–24h expiry, an AI majority/minority summary, and privacy with no social graph**. The space is open, but a decade of poll apps shows why. Polar, Thumb, Wishbone, Jelly, Seesaw and Gas all got heavy engagement and then declined, were acqui-hired or shut down. They failed because of an asker/answerer imbalance, novelty decay, dependence on small friend graphs, and safety or regulatory problems. Opinion's design avoids two of those: it targets strangers, not friends, and it is 18+ and private. But it brings back Jelly's supply problem, and its required reason adds friction that the high-volume apps deliberately avoided. Demand clearly exists. r/AmItheAsshole has 24.5M members, and "making decisions" is 8.5% of ChatGPT messages. So the product has to beat Reddit on speed, privacy and audience targeting, and beat ChatGPT on real human judgment. The science backs the spec's core choices: hide live results, require short reasons, use small diverse crowds, and protect the minority view in summaries. The recommended path is to launch one dense community at a time, mainly college campuses and niche expert groups. Add a vote-to-ask credit, keep the app consumer-free at first, and treat B2B "pulse polls" as the second revenue line. The App Store and legal work is manageable if Opinion is clearly positioned as aggregate polling, not anonymous messaging.

A note on evidence quality: the research was broad but shallow, roughly 10–14 tool calls per topic. Several notes label claims as background knowledge or unverified. This report marks those claims **[unverified]** where they appear.

## Ten years of poll apps grew fast and then disappeared

The pattern is consistent. **Wishbone**, a two-option "compare anything" app for teens, reached **3.1M MAU in about four months** and later 5M MAU with about 25M votes a day. It made money from brand-sponsored polls and was hacked in 2017 ([TechTimes](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/91718/20151006/wishbone-social-barometer-app-skyrockets-among-teens-3-million-plus-users-in-just-four-months.htm); [AdExchanger](https://www.adexchanger.com/mobile/teen-polling-app-wishbone-intersection-advertising-content/); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2017/03/15/teen-quiz-app-wishbone-hacked-users-emails-and-phone-numbers-exposed/?ncid=rss)). **Polar** claimed about 50 votes per user per day on $1.2M of funding and was absorbed into Google+ ([BetaKit](https://betakit.com/polar-adds-1-2m-for-social-voting-app-sees-almost-6m-votes-in-3-months/)). **Thumb** (formerly Opinionaided) ended up inside YPulse, a youth market-research firm ([MrWeb](https://www.mrweb.com/drno/news17486.htm)). That ending is a useful clue: the most durable value in crowd opinion turned out to be research, not social. Seesaw (2013) limited each poll's audience to the asker's contacts ([AllThingsD](http://allthingsd.com/20130207/seesaw-decision-making-app-helps-ask-friends-for-opinions/)). **No Seesaw postmortem or shutdown date was found**, so the claim that its friend graph caused its decline is the notes' own inference.

**Jelly** is the closest warning. After a heavy launch, active users "spiraled down" because most people did not want to ask questions while answers were plentiful ([Macworld](https://www.macworld.com/article/225138/twitter-cofounder-biz-stone-on-jelly-s-spectacular-failure-and-the-yet-to-be-determined-success-of.html)). Opinion has the same two-sided marketplace, but its scarce side is the opposite one. Each poll needs answerers who meet a category filter and will write 20+ characters within hours. **Gas** shows the ceiling and the fragility of viral poll mechanics: 10M+ downloads and about $11M in revenue in roughly three months, a sale to Discord, then shutdown in November 2023 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gas_(app)); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/01/17/discord-acquires-gas-a-compliments-based-social-media-app-for-teens)). Opinion deliberately drops the status loop (likes, followers, "who picked you") that drove Gas and tbh. Whatever keeps users coming back has to come from usefulness instead.

The money has gone to adjacent products. **Fizz** raised $41.5M across 300+ colleges and **Blind** raised $74.9M with 5M+ verified professionals ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/02/06/fizz-brings-on-tiktok-alum-to-help-build-out-its-marketplace-and-recommendation-engine/); [Tracxn](https://tracxn.com/d/companies/teamblind/__MrO4gNXVV5InE3po33SuUzF0wqD6Qhdg1uMpHSaOxr4)). Prediction markets went further: Kalshi was reportedly seeking about a $40B valuation in June 2026 ([CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/business/2026/06/24/kalshi-targets-a-massive-usd40-billion-valuation-widening-lead-over-rival-polymarket)). The consumer poll app Polls Platform raised only about $1.17M ([Tracxn](https://tracxn.com/d/companies/pollsplatform/__qZlNqY6hxhg3ac4zLANmxjGDnvap3Wftx5BHDvm-8EA)). The takeaway is that **verified communities are what investors fund**, and Opinion's "curated communities" should borrow Fizz's .edu check and Blind's work-email verification. Several named rivals (Deciderr, Choicer, Hint, Wiser, OneVote, Swayy) **could not be verified at all**. A figure of $150B+ combined prediction-market volume is **[unverified]**: it came from a search summary only. Not finding a direct 2024–2026 competitor does not prove none exists, so an App Store and Product Hunt sweep is still needed.

## The science supports hidden results, short reasons and protected minorities

The spec's rule of no live percentages is well supported. In Lorenz et al., showing peers' estimates narrowed the spread of answers and raised confidence without improving accuracy ([PNAS](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1008636108)). Salganik's MusicLab found that visible download counts made success more unequal and less predictable ([Science](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.1121066)). Muchnik et al. found that **one random early up-vote raised final ratings by about 25%** ([Science](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.1240466)). The counterpoint is Becker et al.: in decentralized networks where everyone has equal influence, exchanging information improved accuracy ([PNAS](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1615978114)). That condition does not apply to a fast binary poll, so hiding results stays the right default. The science notes say only some of these citations were verified live during the session. The Salganik, Muchnik, Mannes, Prelec, Lerner & Tetlock, Wilson & Schooler and Sweeney citations come **from background knowledge with canonical URLs [unverified this session]**.

On crowd composition, Mannes, Soll and Larrick found that **a small, selected crowd of about five of the best judges** beats both the whole crowd and the single best judge ([APA](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0036677)). The "surprisingly popular" method asks each voter what they think others will pick, and it recovers correct minority answers when experts are outnumbered ([Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature21054)). Self-declared expertise is a weak signal. So "self-selected expert" categories should work as a targeting and display filter, not as a vote weight. Requiring reasons has mixed effects. Accountability to an audience whose views are unknown makes people think more carefully ([APA](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.125.2.255)), but analyzing reasons can make taste judgments worse ([APA](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.60.2.181)). For "which outfit" or "which logo" polls, a required reason may cost both participation and accuracy.

Research on LLM summarization confirms the AI layer works and shows where it is biased. The Habermas Machine study (>5,000 participants) found AI group statements were preferred over human mediators' on quality and fairness, **but support still shifted toward majority positions** ([Science](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.adq2852)). Small et al. warn that LLMs hallucinate and drop content when summarizing deliberation ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.11932)). Open pipelines such as Jigsaw's Sensemaking tools and Talk to the City link every claim back to a source comment ([GitHub](https://github.com/Jigsaw-Code/sensemaking-tools); [GitHub](https://github.com/AIObjectivesInstitute/talk-to-the-city-reports)). Voter reasons are untrusted input. Prompt injection is OWASP's top LLM risk, and no single mitigation fully stops it ([OWASP](https://genai.owasp.org/llmrisk/llm01-prompt-injection/)). Small polls also create a re-identification risk. Sweeney showed that **ZIP code, birth date and sex identify about 87% of Americans** ([Sweeney](https://doi.org/10.1142/S0218488502001648)), and CMS suppresses data cells smaller than 11 ([CMS](https://resdac.org/articles/cms-cell-size-suppression-policy)). The spec's threshold of five votes for a whole poll is too low to protect a minority of two or three people.

## Compliance is manageable if Opinion reads as polling, not anonymous chat

Apple guideline 1.2 requires UGC apps to provide filtering, reporting with timely responses, user blocking and published contact info. It also states that apps built for "random or anonymous chat" may be removed ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)). Opinion's biggest App Review risk is a reviewer classifying it as an anonymous social app. The answers are to keep it strictly aggregate (no DMs, no replies, no voter identities), show reasons only after moderation, and describe it to reviewers as anonymous polling. Under guideline 4.8, offering Google sign-in requires also offering Sign in with Apple; the spec already includes both. Under 5.1.1(v), account deletion must be available in the app. Apple's new **13+/16+/18+ rating questionnaire** blocks updates until it is completed ([MacObserver](https://www.macobserver.com/news/apple-adds-new-app-store-age-ratings-13-16-and-18/)). The Texas and Utah app-store age laws require developers to use the age brackets the stores pass to them ([Baker Data Counsel](https://www.bakerdatacounsel.com/blogs/an-app-developers-guide-to-app-store-age-assurance-laws/)). The note that the Fifth Circuit allowed Texas enforcement in late May 2026 comes from a search summary and is **[unverified]**.

The NGL case sets the regulatory baseline. NGL paid **$5M**, was banned from serving users under 18, and was barred from misleading claims about AI moderation, after sending fake messages and selling a "reveal" subscription that revealed nothing ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/07/ftc-order-will-ban-ngl-labs-its-founders-offering-anonymous-messaging-apps-kids-under-18-halt)). Opinion's 18+ gate and honest engagement design match that lesson. The EU AI Act's Art. 50 transparency duties have applied since **Aug 2, 2026**; the delay covered only the high-risk rules ([Jones Walker](https://www.joneswalker.com/en/insights/blogs/ai-law-blog/yes-august-2-still-matters-the-eu-approved-a-high-risk-ai-delay-but-most-trans.html?id=102nbon)). Labeling every summary as AI-generated, which the spec already does, is the cheap way to comply. The following are **[unverified]** and were flagged by the notes as background knowledge that needs primary-source checks: COPPA's amended-rule timing; that GDPR treats reasons as pseudonymous rather than anonymous; that a DPIA is needed for sensitive opinions; the DSA micro-enterprise exemptions; the UK Online Safety Act's expectation of "highly effective age assurance"; Google Play's UGC and web-deletion rules; and the case histories of Yik Yak, Secret, Gas and Fizz.

Moderation costs little at MVP scale. OpenAI's omni-moderation endpoint is **free** ([OpenAI Help](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/4936833-is-the-moderation-endpoint-free-to-use)). Hive charges **$0.50 per 1,000 requests**, about $500 a month at one million reasons ([Hive](https://thehive.ai/pricing)). **Perspective API shuts down Dec 31, 2026** ([Perspective](https://www.perspectiveapi.com/)), so Opinion should not build on it. Human moderator rates of $5–35/hour are **[unverified]**.

## Growth has to be campus-by-campus, and revenue later comes from research buyers

Andrew Chen's "atomic network" idea fits Opinion's 3–24h window well. Tinder needed about 500 users on one campus, and the "hard side" of a network has to be won first ([Food On Demand](https://foodondemand.com/01062022/andrew-chen-examines-the-cold-start-problem-for-platforms-like-uber-snackpass/); [Buteau](https://www.antoinebuteau.com/lessons-from-andrew-chen/)). Fizz reached about 10% of Stanford undergrads in its first week and eventually 95%, with no paid marketing ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/10/04/fizz-app-college-stanford-social/); [Stanford Daily](https://stanforddaily.com/2022/10/05/stanford-startup-fizz-secures-4-5-million-in-seed-funding-targets-nationwide-expansion/)). Gas ran one-school-at-a-time Instagram launches, but its peak figures come from secondary sources and are **[unverified/unaudited]** ([Synergy Labs](https://www.synergylabs.co/blog/how-nikita-bier-built-two-viral-apps-without-spending-a-dollar-on-marketing)). Paid growth does not work at this stage. Installs cost **$2.88–4.00** on TikTok and Meta ([Business of Apps](https://www.businessofapps.com/marketplace/user-acquisition/research/user-acquisition-costs/)), and a16z recommends that 80–90% of early growth be organic. a16z's targets for a "good" social app are **D1 60% / D7 40% / D30 25%** ([a16z](https://a16z.com/do-you-have-lightning-in-a-bottle-how-to-benchmark-your-social-app/)), against category medians of about 26% D1 and 5% D30 ([UXCam](https://uxcam.com/blog/mobile-app-retention-benchmarks/); the aggregator data is lower confidence). No retention benchmarks specific to poll apps were found, and the notes found **no primary evidence on how well vote-to-ask credit systems perform**.

The best first segment is college students. Campuses give a dense atomic network and cover purchase, career and relationship questions: 18–24-year-olds regularly seek career (77%) and relationship (66%) advice ([Pew](https://www.pewresearch.org/social-trends/2024/01/25/young-adults-relationship-with-their-parents/)). Pre-purchase shoppers and early-career professionals in niche expert categories come next. On monetization, ads clash with a private app that has no feed. Consumer freemium is plausible, but the notes found **no conversion or willingness-to-pay data**. The stronger long-term line is B2B: two options plus reasons plus an AI summary is effectively a fast concept test. Pollfish charges about **$1–3 per complete** ([SurveyMars](https://surveymars.com/knowledge/pollfish-pricing-analysis-is-it-cost-effective-for-market-research/)), Prolific adds a 42.8% corporate fee ([Koji](https://www.koji.so/blog/prolific-pricing-2026)), and the insights industry is worth over $150B ([Research World](https://researchworld.com/articles/inside-the-153bn-insights-industry)). Thumb's sale to YPulse suggests this is where the category's value has always ended up. The risk is trust: B2B polls need explicit opt-in and clear labeling, or they undermine the privacy promise.

## Recommended spec changes

The table turns the findings into concrete edits to SPEC.md. Items marked [P] in the spec that the evidence supports should be signed off as written: hidden live results, no early end, the creator cannot vote, 18+ only, labeled AI output, a pre-AI moderation filter, and report and block.

| Area | Current spec | Recommended change | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| Supply / cold start | No answerer incentive | Add vote-to-ask credits (e.g., answer 3 polls to post 1). Send answerers "your reason was featured" and "creator's pick" feedback. Founders seed polls in each launch community | Jelly imbalance; Chen's hard side |
| Launch scope | Open categories + admin-curated communities | Launch with 1–3 verified campus communities (.edu email) and 2–3 expert categories; hide categories with too few voters to answer in time | Fizz/Blind; atomic network |
| Min threshold | <5 votes = no summary | Results and summary at ≥10 votes; minority summary and minority insights only when the minority has ≥5 reasons, otherwise "a few people disagreed"; show N | k-anonymity, Mannes small crowds |
| Reasons | Required for Expert (20–200), optional for Community | Keep required for advice/judgment polls; offer a "taste" poll type where the reason is optional; cap at 200 chars; block names and @handles | Wilson & Schooler; harassment lessons |
| Expertise | Self-selected categories | Use only to target and to label quotes ("from people who chose Finance"); never to weight votes | Weak self-calibration |
| Optional signal | None | An optional "what will most people pick?" question, used for "surprisingly popular" insights | Prelec 2017 |
| AI pipeline | Summary + 3 insights | Group reasons by side → cluster → separate length budgets for majority and minority → insights chosen for diversity, at least one from the minority when it qualifies → featured quotes must match stored text exactly → reason-ID grounding check → judge for omissions; counts computed in code; reasons wrapped as delimited data; no tools in the summary call | Habermas majority drift; OWASP LLM01 |
| Reveal timing | Results after close | Never show reasons, leanings or vote counts before close, including to the creator beyond a raw count | Herding studies |
| Age ranges | Minimum 5-year width | Keep; also suppress age-range targeting when the estimated audience is under about 50 | Re-identification risk |
| Moderation | Filter + queue | OpenAI omni-moderation first pass plus an injection/PII filter; no Perspective API; 24h report SLA; make only accurate public claims about AI moderation | Cost data; NGL order |
| Store / legal | Stages 2–3 | 18+ rating; read Apple/Google age signals in TX/UT; App Review notes say "anonymous polling, no messaging"; publish contact info; DPIA and DPAs with LLM vendors; EU AI-generated labels | Apple 1.2/4.8/5.1.1; AI Act Art. 50 |
| Metrics | Vote rate, time to first vote, D7 | Add % of polls reaching ≥10 reasoned votes before close (the key liquidity metric), the asker-to-answerer ratio, and a D30 target of ≥25% | a16z benchmarks |
| Monetization | None for MVP | Stay free for MVP; test freemium boosts (wider audience, longer window) after liquidity is proven; plan an opt-in B2B pulse-poll line priced against Pollfish at $1–3 per response | Pollfish/Prolific; Thumb exit |

## Conclusion

Opinion's real product is not the poll. It is a reliable promise that **about 10 relevant strangers will give a reasoned answer within hours**. Every earlier app optimized for vote volume and lost the users once the novelty wore off. Opinion makes the opposite bet, trading volume for reasoning that an AI can turn into something more useful than a percentage. That bet only pays off if each poll actually gets enough answers, so the most important measure is the share of polls that close with enough reasoned votes, not downloads. Track it per community from day one, and do not open a new community until the current one reaches it.

The second implication is strategic. The features that make Opinion trustworthy (hidden results, aggregated anonymity, a protected minority view, AI summaries tied to real quotes) are the same features a research buyer would pay for. Building them carefully from the MVP keeps the B2B option available without changing the consumer product later. The main open questions are retention for reason-required polls, whether users will pay, and the status of the unverified legal points. Answer them with a single-campus pilot and a legal review, then broaden the build.
