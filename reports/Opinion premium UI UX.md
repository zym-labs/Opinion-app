# Make the verdict moment feel earned

Opinion's premium upgrade should not come from a new visual style. It should come from three moments the current "Calm verdict" system already defines and leaves underpowered: the one-tap vote, the one-time results reveal, and the AI summary. The Stage 6 system (flat surfaces, 1px borders, Inter with Source Serif 4 for quotes, A = Indigo and B = Amber) is the right base, and the 2025–2026 trends support it more than they challenge it. Apple's Liquid Glass is optional for App Store review and comes free on native chrome. Material 3 Expressive's spring motion tests best with 18–24 year-olds, who are Opinion's launch audience. The research points to five changes. Let the system draw glass on chrome only. Move all motion to critically damped springs. Turn the result screen into a short, tap-through story with a prediction payoff. Label the AI summary specifically and tie it to the quotes it drew on, giving the minority view equal weight. Replace likes with private mastery signals and credit rules that are always visible. Expo SDK 57 supports all of this through `expo-glass-effect`, Expo Router native tabs, Reanimated 4.5 and `expo-haptics`. The evidence is uneven. Platform and regulatory facts are well sourced. Gen Z statistics and engagement numbers mostly come from vendor or aggregator blogs and should be treated as directional, and the craft claims about apps like Things, Linear and Revolut are unsourced.

## Keep glass on the chrome and put springs everywhere else

Liquid Glass shipped with iOS 26 in September 2025 and is Apple's biggest visual change since iOS 7 ([9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2025/11/06/apple-spotlights-third-party-apps-adopting-liquid-glass-in-ios-26-and-more/)). Apple's gallery of adopting apps now includes Fantastical, Trello and AllTrails ([MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/04/06/apple-liquid-glass-design-gallery-update/)). The claim that adoption is **not required for App Store review** comes from a secondary practitioner post ([Medium](https://medium.com/@saianbusekar/liquid-glass-ui-in-ios-26-no-panic-no-rush-no-app-store-risk-3a26f352a946)). There was also a backlash: secondary reports put early iOS 26 uptake around 15%, and the figures vary by source ([Notebookcheck](https://www.notebookcheck.net/iOS-26-reportedly-struggling-for-adoption-amid-Liquid-Glass-UI-backlash.1201110.0.html)). For Opinion, the low-risk move is to let the system draw glass on tab bars, headers and form sheets and keep poll cards and option tiles opaque. Glass behind question text and serif quotes would hurt legibility, which matters most in an app built on reading. The notes did not verify Apple's Liquid Glass HIG page, its Reduce Transparency guidance, or whether iOS 27 changed anything. Check those before building.

Motion matters more than glass. Material 3 Expressive replaces duration-based easing with springs defined by stiffness and damping ratio. It splits them into "spatial" springs for movement and "effect" springs for color and opacity, and a spring can be retargeted mid-flight without a visible jump ([M3 blog](https://m3.material.io/blog/m3-expressive-motion-theming)). Google's research covered **46 studies and more than 18,000 participants**. Expressive designs were preferred across ages, most strongly by 18–24 year-olds (up to 87% in some tests), and users found key elements up to 4x faster ([Google Design](https://design.google/library/expressive-material-design-google-research)). Opinion should take M3E's springs and its large, high-contrast touch targets, but not its playful shape-morphing, which would undercut the "calm" direction. In practice, define one spatial spring and one effect spring in `packages/shared/tokens.ts` for both platforms. Replace the result bars' 600ms ease-out with a critically damped spring (damping ratio 1, no overshoot) so a fast tap-through never looks broken. Keep Reduce Motion as an instant change or a fade.

Haptics should follow Apple's rule: use them consistently, sparingly, and only to reinforce visible feedback, never in place of it ([Apple HIG](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/playing-haptics)). That gives a short map. `selectionAsync()` on option select, `notificationAsync(Success)` on the final vote, one `impactAsync(Light)` when the result bars settle, `notificationAsync(Error)` on validation failure, and nothing on scrolling, feed taps or the countdown. The one transition worth custom work is a shared-element-style move where a feed card's A/B mini tiles grow into the vote screen's option tiles. The case for this rests on general practice (it is often credited to Airbnb), not on anything sourced in the notes.

## Expo SDK 57 already ships the toolkit, with a few traps

SDK 57 (June 30, 2026) is a non-breaking release on React Native 0.86 that bundles **Reanimated 4.5, Worklets 0.10 and Gesture Handler 2.32**. Pin to at least **expo@57.0.17**, which fixes a Hermes V1 memory regression that affected apps importing Reanimated or Worklets ([Expo changelog](https://expo.dev/changelog/sdk-57)). `expo-glass-effect` provides `GlassView` (`glassEffectStyle` `'clear' | 'regular' | 'none'`, `tintColor`, `isInteractive`) and `GlassContainer` (whose `spacing` prop controls when shapes merge). Gate it with `isLiquidGlassAvailable()` and `isGlassEffectAPIAvailable()`. It falls back to a plain `View` below iOS 26. One trap: **setting opacity to 0 on a GlassView or any of its parents stops the glass from rendering**, so fade-ins must animate a child or use a different technique ([Expo docs](https://docs.expo.dev/versions/latest/sdk/glass-effect/)).

Native tabs render Liquid Glass automatically on iOS 26. They support badges, which fits the existing "results ready" badge on Feed, and `minimizeBehavior="onScrollDown"`. On SDK 57 the docs indicate the import is `expo-router/unstable-native-tabs`, with the stable path arriving in SDK 58. Opinion's four tabs fit Android's five-tab limit. The tab bar height cannot be measured, so bottom padding has to come from safe-area or content insets, not a hard-coded number ([Expo docs](https://docs.expo.dev/router/advanced/native-tabs/)). Reanimated 4's CSS-style animations and transitions work across platforms ([Reanimated docs](https://docs.swmansion.com/react-native-reanimated/docs/css-animations/animation-name/)) and suit simple state changes like tile border, tile scale and the countdown color flip. Shared values with `withSpring` suit the bars and the percentage count-up. The notes recommend several things they did not source: skip Skia unless you build one hero visual such as a split ring; use `expo-blur` only as the pre-iOS-26 fallback and never over long Android lists; embed fonts with the expo-font config plugin; ship static Inter weights because variable-axis support in React Native was not verified; and target 60fps on mid-range Android. The claim that Reanimated 4 requires the New Architecture is also unverified.

## The reveal should be a once-only story, not a chart

Because a result can be viewed only once, the reveal is the emotional peak of the product, and the current spec treats it as a static screen. The proven format is Spotify Wrapped, which in 2019 moved from a microsite to an **in-app, full-screen, tap-through story sequence shareable to Instagram Stories in one tap** ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spotify_Wrapped)). The notes suggest six beats. First, a "Your poll closed" sealed card. Second, a prediction payoff that reuses the existing PredictionPicker answer ("You guessed A — let's see"). Third, the split bar springing from 50/50 to the real numbers over roughly 1.2 seconds, with one haptic. Fourth, a "The room said…" AI summary card. Fifth, two or three reasons in serif. Sixth, a share card. Starting from 50/50 instead of from zero (the current spec) frames the result as a contest that resolves, not a quantity that fills up. No peer-reviewed data on reveal timing was found, so the 1.2 seconds is a starting point to test. The result is uncertain by nature, so no extra random reward is needed.

For the data visual, Polymarket offers the right anatomy: one large percentage and a two-color bar, with hierarchy carried by saturation on a quiet canvas ([Avark Agency](https://avark.agency/learn/prediction-market-design-patterns)). Do not copy its green/red Yes/No colors, which signal right and wrong. Keep Indigo/Amber plus letters. Do not add trophies, confetti or "winner" styling. Pol.is is the model here: it shows minority opinion groups as clearly as majority ones and reports consensus and division separately ([Participedia](https://participedia.net/en/methods/polis); [Open Rights Group](https://www.openrightsgroup.org/publications/democratic-innovations-polis-and-the-political-process/)). The share card should follow Wordle's spoiler-free principle. Wordle's colored grid showed performance without revealing the answer, and the game spread after the share feature was built in ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wordle)). For Opinion, that means the split and an AI one-liner, never anything that could identify the asker or voters.

View-once creates an accessibility obligation the current spec does not cover. A screen-reader user must hear the whole result before it is consumed. Each story card should expose a complete label (for example, "Option A, MacBook Air, 62 percent, 23 of 37 votes, majority, your pick"). The "seen" state should commit only when the user leaves the final card, not when the first frame renders. Haptics must stay optional. Under Reduce Motion, bar growth becomes a fade. WCAG 2.2 adds 2.5.8 Target Size (24×24 CSS px minimum) and 2.4.11 Focus Not Obscured to the existing 1.4.1 Use of Color and 1.4.3 contrast requirements ([W3C](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/)). Apple asks for 44×44pt targets, Dynamic Type and Reduce Motion support ([Apple HIG](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility)). The notes did not re-fetch either page, and Android TalkBack behavior for charts was not checked.

## Label the AI summary specifically and show its sources

The evidence on AI labels pulls in two directions, and it shapes how the AISummary component should look. Bare "AI-generated" labels **lower perceived credibility even when the content is accurate**, which researchers call the transparency dilemma ([AAAI AIES](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AIES/article/download/36671/38809/40746)). AI labels also lowered the perceived authenticity of user reviews ([MDPI JTAER](https://www.mdpi.com/0718-1876/21/5/154)). On the other hand, Trusting News found that audience comfort **rose as disclosures included more detail** ([Trusting News](https://trustingnews.org/new-research-how-ai-disclosures-in-news-help-and-also-hurt-trust-with-audiences/); [arXiv 2601.09620](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.09620)). Talk to the City shows the pattern that resolves this: LLM-clustered arguments, each linked back to the source quotes ([AI Objectives Institute](https://ai.objectives.institute/blog/introducing-talk-to-the-city-our-collective-deliberation-tool)). A secondary aggregator reports that about 63% of Gen Z see AI as potentially inauthentic ([Supercharged Studio](https://www.supercharged.studio/blog/gen-z-design-trends)). That is low-authority, but it fits a better-sourced finding that Gen Z rank authenticity above every other brand value tested ([Marketing Dive](https://www.marketingdive.com/news/gen-z-wants-brands-to-be-fun-authentic-and-good-study-says/581191/)).

Regulation removes the option of not disclosing. **EU AI Act Article 50 became enforceable on 2 August 2026.** Deployers that publish AI text to inform the public on matters of public interest must disclose it unless it is substantively human-reviewed, and fines reach EUR 15M or 3% of turnover ([Orrick](https://www.orrick.com/en/Insights/2026/08/EU-AI-Act-Transparency-Obligations-for-AI-Generated-Content-Article-50); [EC FAQ](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/faqs/transparency-obligations-under-article-50-ai-act)). Whether a campus decision poll counts as "public interest" is unclear, so the conservative course is to always disclose.

That leads to concrete changes. Replace the generic "AI summary" tag and trailing disclaimer with a specific header line: "Written by AI from 37 voters' reasons." Give majority and minority sections identical card size and type, varying only the A/B stripe, label and percentage. Attach a small citation chip to each summary point that scrolls to the supporting anonymous quote. Among the three featured quotes, always include at least one from the minority, and state the selection rule in a "How this works" sheet. That sheet follows Microsoft HAX's guidance to make clear what the system can do and why it did what it did, and Google PAIR's guidance on calibrating trust ([HAX](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/haxtoolkit/ai-guidelines/); [PAIR](https://pair.withgoogle.com/guidebook/)), though the notes did not re-fetch either. When one side has few reasons, show a "Only a few reasons so far" state instead of confident prose, and add a "Report this summary" action. Keep the existing contrast: human words in warm serif, AI text in sans on `surface-muted`. The notes suggest that Perplexity's inline citations and Apple's distinct glyph for notification summaries are useful precedents, but those UI details were not re-sourced.

## Show anonymity at the moment of writing and make the rules visible

Anonymity changes what people say. In one study, **74% admitted cheating under anonymity versus 25% under confidentiality** ([Ong & Weiss](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/280778863_The_impact_of_anonymity_on_responses_to_sensitive_questions)). But complete anonymity sometimes increased low-effort answering ([Lelkes, Krosnick et al.](https://web.stanford.edu/dept/communication/faculty/krosnick/docs/2012/Anonymity%20JESP%20FINAL%20June%202012.pdf)). Belief about anonymity matters as much as the reality ([NCSU](https://repository.lib.ncsu.edu/bitstreams/3bc2ea76-0bec-4db2-af54-588eea2f4951/download)). So the privacy cue belongs directly under the ReasonField, worded concretely: "Your name is never shown. Your reason may be quoted anonymously — leave out details that identify you." It should replace the separate lock line at the bottom of the vote screen and pair with automatic PII scrubbing. Opinion's light accountability mechanisms (one vote per poll, a 20-character minimum, an 18+ gate) already counter the satisficing risk.

For gamification, the relevant failure is Gas. Gas wrote every prompt, distributed votes so that, it claimed, over 95% of users who added friends got a compliment on day one, and was shut down about nine months after Discord bought it ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gas_(app)); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/10/19/discord-kills-gas-anonymous-compliments-app-bought-nine-months-ago/)). Critics said it "monetizes their anxiety" ([CBC](https://amp.cbc.ca/lite/story/1.6718229)). Opinion should copy the distribution mechanic, routing voters toward polls that are short of votes so every asker gets an answer, but keep a real decision as the core loop, not novelty. Hiding counts costs little. When Instagram tested hiding likes, Mosseri said it "didn't actually change nearly as much" ([Platformer](https://www.platformer.news/instagrams-big-likes-anticlimax/)). Private mastery signals can replace likes: "You matched the majority 7 of 10 times", a Contrarian marker, and "Your reason was quoted in a summary". Duolingo's figures (streak freezes reportedly cutting at-risk churn 21%, and 7-day streakers retaining 2.4x better) come only from secondary teardowns ([StriveCloud](https://www.strivecloud.io/blog/gamification-examples-boost-user-retention-duolingo)). If Opinion adds streaks, make them weekly, include a built-in freeze, and avoid loss-framed copy. The legal limit is EU DSA Article 25, which bars interfaces that "deceive or manipulate" users; a 2022 EU sweep found **97% of popular sites and apps used at least one dark pattern** ([ACM CHI](https://dl.acm.org/doi/full/10.1145/3772318.3791479)). BeReal's reciprocity gate, where you post before you can see others' posts ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.02883v1)), is the honest framing for credits: "Give 3 opinions, get 1 ask", always shown on the CreditPill and never hidden.

## Screen-by-screen upgrades, in priority order

Onboarding and empty states come first, because the first session decides retention. Social apps reportedly keep about 40% of users on Day 1 at the median and 50–60% at the top ([UXCam](https://uxcam.com/blog/mobile-app-retention-benchmarks/)). Vendor blogs report that priming before the system notification prompt lifts opt-in 2–3x ([MobiLoud](https://www.mobiloud.com/blog/push-notification-opt-in-rate/)), and that a soft ask with "remind me later" lifts acceptance 30–50% ([Pushwoosh](https://www.pushwoosh.com/blog/increase-push-notifications-opt-in/)). These are vendor numbers, not audited data. The table below applies the evidence to the Stage 6 system.

| Priority | Screen / component | Upgrade | Expo / RN implementation |
|---|---|---|---|
| P0 | Result (F-05) | Story sequence: sealed card → prediction payoff → spring split from 50/50 → equal-weight majority/minority AI cards with citation chips → quotes → share card. Commit "viewed" on exit of the last card | Pager with Gesture Handler taps; `withSpring` (damping ratio 1) for bars; `impactAsync(Light)` on settle; per-card `accessibilityLabel`; fade under `useReducedMotion()` |
| P0 | AISummary | Specific header ("Written by AI from N voters' reasons"), low-N uncertainty state, "How this works" sheet, report action, mandatory minority quote | Existing `surface-muted` + `ai` token; chips as `Pressable` scrolling to quote refs |
| P0 | Onboarding | Age gate → 3 seeded campus polls voted with no signup wall → account → first ask → soft notification ask tied to the reveal | Platform-written seed polls; pre-permission sheet before `expo-notifications` request |
| P1 | Vote (F-02) | Single-tap commit with the reason sheet sliding up after selection; privacy line moved under ReasonField; haptic map | Reanimated CSS transitions for tile select; sheet on 250ms → spatial spring token; `selectionAsync` / `notificationAsync` |
| P1 | Navigation chrome | Native tabs with Feed results badge; system glass on iOS 26, `expo-blur` fallback below | `expo-router/unstable-native-tabs` on SDK 57; inset-based bottom padding; glass only on chrome |
| P1 | Feed / EmptyState | Never empty: seeded campus polls; feed routes voters to polls short of votes; card → vote shared-element transition | One-column list; Reanimated layout transitions |
| P2 | Profile | Private mastery stats (majority-match rate, Contrarian, quoted-in-summary) in a small bento grid; weekly forgiving streak if added | Plain Views; tabular figures; no public counts |
| P2 | CreditPill | Always-visible "give 3, get 1" rule; count-up on earn; no fake urgency | `withSpring` count-up; explanation sheet |
| P2 | Share card | Wordle-style spoiler-free: split + AI one-liner, no identity | `react-native-view-shot` or server render at 1080×1350 |
| Ongoing | Tokens / a11y | Add spatial and effect spring tokens; fix `option-b` and `text-faint` contrast before shipping the CI test | `packages/shared/tokens.ts`; `tokens.test.ts` |

The notes leave several gaps that should be closed before final design. There was no app-by-app evidence from Mobbin for Things, Linear, Headspace, Revolut or Apple Invites, so any "feel like Things" target is a hypothesis. There were no primary Gen Z reports from Pew or Snap. There was no study on AI summaries of crowd opinion specifically, so the trust findings are borrowed from news and reviews. And Apple's Liquid Glass HIG and the iOS 27 changes were not checked.

## Conclusion

Opinion's restraint is an advantage worth keeping. The 2026 trends reward polished motion, honest AI and visible privacy more than visual spectacle, and the "Calm verdict" system already has the right ingredients. What it lacks is a sense of occasion at the moments that are scarce by design. A result can be seen once, a vote is final, and a reason may be quoted. Treat each of those as a moment with its own motion, haptic and copy, and the app can feel premium without adding glass to content or social counters to the feed.

The most distinctive opportunity is in the AI layer. Most products either hide AI or decorate it with sparkles. Opinion can make the summary accountable instead: a specific label, citations back to real reasons, and a minority view given equal space. That answers Gen Z skepticism, satisfies Article 50, and makes the product's core promise, that you heard real people, something users can check in the interface.
